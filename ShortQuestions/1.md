**What are the three pillars of observability, and what is the primary purpose of each?**

The three pillars of observability are metrics, logs, and traces, and each serves a distinct purpose in understanding and operating modern systems.

**Metrics** are numerical measurements collected over time that provide a high-level view of system health and performance. They are typically aggregated values such as CPU usage, memory consumption, request rate, error rate, or latency percentiles. The primary purpose of metrics is monitoring and alerting. They help teams quickly detect when something is wrong—for example, when error rates spike or latency increases. Because they are lightweight and efficient to store, metrics are ideal for dashboards, trend analysis, and service-level objective (SLO) tracking. However, while they indicate that a problem exists, they usually do not explain why it is happening.

**Logs** are detailed records of events that occur within an application or system. They may be structured (e.g., JSON) or unstructured text and often include error messages, stack traces, user actions, or configuration changes. The primary purpose of logs is debugging and root cause analysis. When a system fails, logs provide the contextual information needed to understand what happened and why. They are especially valuable for troubleshooting specific incidents and for auditing or security investigations. Unlike metrics, logs offer depth and detail, but they can generate large volumes of data and require indexing or search tools to analyze effectively.

**Traces** track the lifecycle of a request as it flows through multiple services in a distributed system. They show how long each component takes to process the request and how services interact with one another. The primary purpose of traces is to diagnose latency issues and failures in distributed architectures, such as microservices environments. Traces make it possible to pinpoint which service or dependency is causing delays or errors. While metrics reveal performance trends and logs explain individual events, traces connect those pieces by showing where in the system a problem occurs.

Together, metrics, logs, and traces provide complementary visibility. Metrics detect issues, traces locate them within distributed systems, and logs explain them in detail.
