**Explain the three states of a Circuit Breaker (CLOSED, OPEN, HALF-OPEN) and how transitions between them work.**

A Circuit Breaker is a resilience pattern used in distributed systems to prevent cascading failures when calling remote services (e.g., REST APIs, databases, microservices). It acts like an automatic switch that monitors failures and temporarily stops calls to an unhealthy dependency. The three core states are CLOSED, OPEN, and HALF-OPEN, and the system transitions between them based on failure metrics and time.

In the CLOSED state, the circuit breaker behaves normally: all requests are allowed to pass through to the target service. While in this state, it continuously monitors outcomes such as timeouts, exceptions, and error responses. If the failure rate exceeds a configured threshold (for example, 50% failures over the last 20 requests), the circuit breaker determines that the dependency is unhealthy. Once this threshold is crossed, the breaker transitions from CLOSED to OPEN. The goal here is early detection of instability before it spreads system-wide.

In the OPEN state, the circuit breaker immediately rejects all incoming calls without attempting to contact the failing service. Instead of allowing requests to hit a likely failing dependency, it fails fast—often returning a fallback response or error immediately. This protects system resources (threads, connections, CPU) and prevents cascading failures. The breaker remains OPEN for a configured “cool-down” or “sleep window” period. During this time, it does not attempt real calls to the dependency.

After the cool-down period expires, the circuit breaker transitions to the HALF-OPEN state. In HALF-OPEN, it allows a limited number of test requests to pass through to the underlying service. This is essentially a probe phase to check whether the service has recovered. If these trial requests succeed (based on configured success criteria), the breaker transitions back to CLOSED and resumes normal traffic. However, if the trial requests fail, the breaker immediately transitions back to OPEN and restarts the cool-down timer.
