**In single-leader replication, what is the split-brain problem during failover, and why is it dangerous?**

The split-brain problem in single-leader replication occurs during failover when the leader node becomes unavailable (or is suspected to be down) and multiple replicas mistakenly assume they should become the new leader. This leads to a situation where two or more nodes think they are the leader at the same time.

1. How it happens

- In a single-leader system, one node handles all writes, and replicas follow it.

- If the leader fails, the system triggers failover to promote a replica to leader.

- If network partitions or communication failures occur, multiple replicas may not know the status of each other, and each may promote itself to leader.

- This creates two independent leaders, each accepting writes.

2. Why it’s dangerous

- Data divergence: Each leader can accept different writes, leading to inconsistent states across replicas.

- Lost updates: When the partition resolves, merging data can be extremely difficult or impossible without conflicts.

- System reliability issues: Clients may read inconsistent data depending on which “leader” they connect to.

- Potential data corruption: Automatic resolution may overwrite valid updates from one leader with updates from another.
