**How does the Kubernetes Scheduler decide which node to place a Pod on? What factors does it consider?**

The Kubernetes Scheduler is responsible for deciding which node a new Pod should run on, and it does this through a two-step process: filtering (predicates) and scoring (priorities). First, the scheduler filters out nodes that are unsuitable for the Pod. This step considers constraints such as node resource availability, Pod resource requests, taints and tolerations, node selectors, affinity and anti-affinity rules, and whether the node is already running a Pod with conflicting characteristics. Essentially, any node that cannot meet the Pod's requirements is eliminated from consideration at this stage.

After filtering, the scheduler scores the remaining nodes to find the most optimal placement. Scoring involves evaluating nodes based on a variety of preference criteria. These include spreading Pods evenly across nodes (to avoid hotspots), preferring nodes with more available resources, honoring affinity/anti-affinity rules to keep certain Pods together or apart, and considering historical Pod scheduling behavior. The node with the highest total score becomes the chosen node for the Pod.

In addition to these basic factors, the scheduler also accounts for custom scheduling policies and extenders. Administrators can define policies to prioritize certain nodes, or integrate external schedulers to handle specialized workloads. Overall, the Kubernetes Scheduler balances multiple constraints and preferences to ensure Pods are efficiently placed, resources are utilized effectively, and workloads are resilient across the cluster.
