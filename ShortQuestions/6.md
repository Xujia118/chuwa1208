**Compare Two-Phase Commit (2PC) and the Saga pattern for distributed transactions. What is the main weakness of 2PC that Saga avoids, and what guarantee does Saga sacrifice in return?**

1. Two-Phase Commit (2PC)

How it works:

Prepare phase: The coordinator asks all participants if they can commit. Each participant locks resources and responds with yes or no.

Commit phase:

If all participants vote yes, the coordinator sends commit.

If any participant votes no, the coordinator sends rollback.

Guarantees:

Atomicity: Either all participants commit, or none do.

Strong consistency.

Main weakness:

Blocking: If the coordinator crashes after sending prepare but before sending commit/rollback, participants are stuck waiting indefinitely, holding locks → can lead to resource contention and unavailability.

Also sensitive to network failures.


2. Saga Pattern

How it works:

A long-running transaction is broken into a sequence of local transactions, each with a corresponding compensating transaction to undo it if needed.

If a step fails, previously completed steps are compensated (rolled back) in reverse order.

Guarantees:

Eventual consistency: The system will eventually reach a consistent state if compensations are applied.

Non-blocking: No global locks are held; failures are handled with compensating actions.

Trade-offs:

Sacrifices atomicity: Unlike 2PC, Sagas cannot guarantee all-or-nothing at the same instant. Some partial effects may temporarily exist.

Requires carefully designed compensating actions for each step.


3. Main weakness of 2PC that Saga avoids:

Blocking due to coordinator or participant failures.

Guarantee that Saga sacrifices in return:

Immediate atomicity (all-or-nothing); it only ensures eventual consistency.
