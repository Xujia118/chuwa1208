**Given N (number of partitions) and M (number of consumers,) what will happen when N>=M and N<M respectively?**

When N ≥ M (number of partitions is greater than or equal to number of consumers), every consumer will be assigned at least one partition. If N > M, some consumers will handle multiple partitions. This allows good parallelism and full consumer utilization, since partitions can be distributed across consumers. However, the maximum parallelism is limited by M — you cannot have more actively processing consumers than partitions assigned per consumer.

When N < M (number of partitions is less than number of consumers), some consumers will remain idle because each partition can be consumed by only one consumer within a consumer group at a time. In this case, parallelism is limited by N, not M. Even if you add more consumers, they will not increase throughput unless you increase the number of partitions.

In short, the effective parallelism of a consumer group is min(N, M), and increasing consumers beyond the number of partitions does not improve processing capacity.
